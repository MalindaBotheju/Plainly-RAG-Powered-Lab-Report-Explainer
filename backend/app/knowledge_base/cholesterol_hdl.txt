TEST: HDL Cholesterol ("Good" Cholesterol)

What it measures: The amount of high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, which helps carry excess cholesterol away from artery walls back to the liver.

Why it's tested: Higher HDL is generally associated with lower cardiovascular risk, so unlike LDL, higher is usually better here.

What a low result can mean: Low HDL is associated with higher cardiovascular risk over time. It's often influenced by lack of physical activity, smoking, and some genetic factors.

What a high result can mean: A high HDL is not typically a concern and is usually considered protective.

Typical next steps if abnormal (low): Increasing physical activity and stopping smoking are the most well-supported ways to raise HDL. This is usually looked at together with LDL and triglycerides, not on its own.
