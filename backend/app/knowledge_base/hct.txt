TEST: HCT (Hematocrit)

What it measures: The percentage of your blood's volume that is made up of red blood cells.

Why it's tested: Part of a routine complete blood count (CBC). Used alongside hemoglobin and RBC count to check for anemia (too few red blood cells) or polycythemia (too many), and to help assess hydration status.

What a low result can mean: A low hematocrit usually points to the same causes as low hemoglobin or low RBC count -- anemia from iron or vitamin deficiency, chronic blood loss, kidney disease, or bone marrow problems. It can also happen with overhydration (fluid diluting the blood).

What a high result can mean: A high hematocrit can happen with dehydration (blood is more concentrated), smoking, living at high altitude, or less commonly, a bone marrow condition that overproduces red blood cells.

Typical next steps if abnormal: Usually interpreted together with hemoglobin and RBC count rather than alone. Additional tests (iron studies, vitamin B12/folate, reticulocyte count) and a discussion with a doctor are typical next steps, especially if there are symptoms like fatigue or dizziness.
