TEST: Hemoglobin

What it measures: The protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen around your body.

Why it's tested: Used to check for anemia (low red blood cell / oxygen-carrying capacity) or, less commonly, an excess of red blood cells.

What a low result can mean: Low hemoglobin (anemia) can cause tiredness, weakness, shortness of breath, or pale skin. Common causes include low iron, vitamin deficiencies, chronic blood loss (for example from heavy periods or the digestive tract), or long-term illness. The cause isn't determined by this one number -- more tests (like iron studies) are usually needed.

What a high result can mean: High hemoglobin can occur with dehydration, smoking, living at high altitude, or less commonly, a bone marrow condition.

Typical next steps if abnormal: Additional blood tests to find the cause (iron studies, vitamin B12/folate, reticulocyte count) and a discussion with a doctor, especially if there are symptoms like fatigue or dizziness.
